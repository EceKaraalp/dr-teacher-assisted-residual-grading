General Comments to Associate Editor and Reviewers

Thanks for the guidance which has helped us further improve the paper. We have addressed each comment the reviewers raised and revised the manuscript accordingly. We believe the manuscript has been significantly improved with respect to the aspects identified by the reviewers. Based on the concerns reflected in the comments, we would like to make some general comments:
(1) We have added a new external dataset (D1NAMO) to evaluate generalizability and model robustness, as suggested by the reviewers.
(2) We have included statistical validation using paired t-tests, confidence intervals, and effect sizes to support claims of superiority.
(3) We have enhanced the interpretability section by adding feature importance visualizations.
(4) We have added SEG evaluations for all 12 patients to support clinical relevance claims.
(5) We have revised the ablation study to include parameter counts and analyze complexity–performance trade-offs.
(6) We have further clarified the preprocessing steps, listed the patient IDs used, and detailed the UKS-based smoothing and normalization strategy.
(7) We have added a sensitivity analysis on trend-weight boundaries to justify temporal resolution selection.

Our research presents a robust and innovative solution for real-time glucose prediction, offering a new direction for advancing diabetes management.

A Paired t-test is a statistical method used to compare two related samples or repeated measurements on the same subjects. This approach controls for subject-specific characteristics and provides more robust statistical inference than independent sample comparisons by considering individual variability through paired observations.
The t-statistic is a standardized measure that quantifies the magnitude of the difference between two groups relative to the variability within the data. Larger absolute t-values provide stronger evidence against the null hypothesis of no difference. P-values represent the probability of observing a test statistic as extreme as or more extreme than the one calculated, assuming the null hypothesis is true. A p-value less than 0.05 is conventionally considered statistically significant, indicating strong evidence against the null hypothesis.
95% confidence intervals provide a range of values that likely contains the true population parameter. For mean differences, confidence intervals offer insight into the precision of the estimate and the magnitude of the effect. Wide intervals indicate inaccurate predictions, while small intervals suggest precise predictions.
Cohen's d is a standardized measure of effect size that quantifies the magnitude of the difference between two groups in terms of standard deviation units. Effect sizes are typically interpreted as small (d ≈0.2), medium (d ≈ 0.5), or large (d ≈0.8) with values exceeding 0.8 indicating a very large effect. Effect size measures complement statistical significance testing by indicating whether observed differences are practically meaningful, as statistical significance can be achieved with trivial differences given a large enough sample size.

UKS improves data quality by predicting hidden states and filtering of the series, which removes short-term fluctuations that may hide important trends. Unlike the traditional Kalman filter, which relies on linearization, the unscented Kalman filter uses the unscented transform to propagate the mean and covariance through nonlinear functions without making a linear approximation. These sigma points are passed through the nonlinear system, and their weighted outcomes are used to reconstruct the posterior mean and covariance more accurately.

This table systematically evaluates the individual and combined contributions of key architectural components, including time-aware embeddings, feature importance weighting, multi-resolution fusion, glucose trend-weighting, and unscented Kalman smoothing (UKS).
Each variant was tested under the same 30-minute prediction horizon to ensure comparability. The results demonstrate that removing any of these components results in a noticeable degradation in performance (up to +1.57 mg/dL RMSE), confirming that each plays a distinct and complementary role in overall accuracy. Additionally, we report the standard deviation and parameter count for each configuration to provide further insights into trade-offs between model complexity and predictive performance.
